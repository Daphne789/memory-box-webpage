The Challenge We're Addressing

⚠️ Passive Engagement & Diminished Self Efficacy

During my service work at the senior care center, I noticed that many people were only passively involved. This behavior may indicate underlying psychological barriers that hinder their ability to participate meaningfully.

❶ Decisional Passivity and Self Limiting Beliefs

When asked about preferences during activities or conversations, responses reveal a stepping back from making choices:

"Anything is fine" • "Up to you" • "Whatever is nice"
"I'm already old, listen to what music. If there's music being played then listen along, if not then don't"

This shows stepping back from making choices, not because they don't care, but because they feel their choices don't really matter anymore. It highlights a feeling of powerlessness, where people feel their actions don't make a difference.

❷ Conversational Withdrawal

Engaging in light conversations about hobbies and interests often results in minimal responses. When individuals give short answers without explaining further, the discussions become one sided. This makes it harder to build genuine relationships.

Why This Matters

Cognitive Impact: Making decisions and expressing preferences keeps the mind active by using memory and thinking skills. When these mental activities stop, cognitive abilities can decline more quickly.

Social Impact: When conversations stay surface level without back and forth exchange, real connections cannot form. This leads to deeper feelings of loneliness and isolation.

Identity Impact: When people stop making choices and believing they deserve to enjoy life, they gradually lose connection with who they are as unique individuals.

Sessions Timeline Breakdown

Main Programme: Creating Your Memory Box (90 Minutes)

1
🗣️

Introduction

Time: 5 minutes

Explain the activity: "Today, you'll create a memory box filled with cards representing your life story. Every choice is yours, we're here to help bring your ideas to life."

2
🖼️

Card Selection

Time: 35 minutes

Spread out cards across all 6 categories. Invite participants to select at least 3 cards that resonate with their life. Walk around to assist and gently prompt: "Which food brings back good memories?"

3
🎨

Box Decoration

Time: 30 minutes

Reference their card selections: "You chose the mahjong card, would you like green decorations like the mahjong tiles?" Provide some pre cut materials with adhesive already applied for easy handling.

4
💬

Story Sharing

Time: 20 minutes

Invite volunteers to share why they selected specific cards. If no volunteers, gently prompt individuals. Position them as storytellers whose experiences are valuable.

Ongoing Programme Integration (Week 1 - Week 5)

Week 1

Initial Memory Box Creation

Select some cards, decorate box, share initial stories.

Week 2

Add Drinks and Animals Categories

During other main activity breaks, participants select at least 3 cards from expanded categories. Some share stories from previous selections.

Week 3

Add Family and Singapore Eras Categories

Continue expanding with new categories. Memory boxes grow richer with each session, creating ongoing narrative.

Week 4

Add Natural Landscape and Snacks Categories

Final opportunity for participants to complete their collections with remaining categories. Participants are encouraged to share their completed boxes and reflect on their journey.

Week 5

Special Edition Upgrades

Most engaged 2 - 3 participants in sharing their stories receive music box mechanisms as recognition. All participants have rich, personalized memory collections.

03

Measuring Success

📊 Success Indicators

💬

Conversational Engagement

Participants start conversations on their own more often and talk for longer periods. They begin asking questions and sharing stories without being prompted.

✋

Reduced Passive Responses

Less frequent use of phrases like "anything," "up to you," or "whatever is nice" during activities, tracked through observation logs.

🎯

Preference Expression

Participants show willingness to express preferences in other activities, not just the memory box programme, indicating real shifts in engagement rather than temporary compliance.

😊

Qualitative Feedback

Participants express feeling heard and valued through their feedback. They show pride in their memory boxes and ask to continue working on them or share them with others.

Expected Impact

By positioning elderly participants as decision makers, storytellers, and experts in their own lives, this programme directly addresses learned helplessness and beliefs that limit their engagement. Each memory box becomes a physical reminder that their life, experiences, and choices matter and deserve to be remembered.
